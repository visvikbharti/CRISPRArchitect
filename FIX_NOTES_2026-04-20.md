**Date.** 2026-04-20
**Scope.** Fold the bystander penalty into the ``safety`` dimension instead of
the ``consequence`` TOPSIS dimension. Architectural refactor only — ranking
preserved on the v3 benchmark (30/30 top-1, 30/30 top-3, unchanged).
**Reference.** REVIEW_NOTES_2026-04-18.md §4.2. Complements FIX_NOTES_2026-04-18.md (which shipped Fix #1 — the PE capability gate).

## What changed

Before Fix #2, the coefficient ``bystander_severity × 0.08`` was applied inside
``_compute_consequence_penalty`` — the input to the ``consequence`` TOPSIS
dimension (weight 0.08). The ``_score_safety`` method returned 1.0 for every
0-DSB modality (ABE, CBE, PE) without distinguishing them, which made the
safety axis a ceiling rather than a ranked dimension for non-DSB strategies.

After Fix #2, the same ``bystander_severity × 0.08`` coefficient is applied
inside ``_score_safety`` (dimension weight 0.30). The ``consequence`` TOPSIS
dimension is retained — splice-proximity effects still live there — but
bystanders now contribute where they belong conceptually: to safety.

The refactor implements §4.2 of the external review exactly, and the 30-case
benchmark confirms rank preservation.

### Behavioural contract

- **0-DSB strategies** (ABE, CBE, PE): safety_score = 1.0 − bystander_severity × 0.08. PE (bystander=0) keeps safety=1.0; ABE/CBE with bystander in the 0.3–0.7 range drop to ~0.94–0.98.
- **1-DSB strategies**: safety_score = 0.6 − (p53 ? 0.1 : 0) − bystander × 0.08.
- **2+-DSB strategies**: safety_score = 0.3 − (simultaneous ? 0.1 : 0) − (p53 ? 0.1 : 0) − bystander × 0.08.
- **All branches**: clamp to [0, 1].
- **Consequence dimension** (weight 0.08 in TOPSIS): now captures only splice-proximity penalties (0.05 per near-exon-start or near-exon-end proximity), capped at 0.3.
- **Consequence bonus** (``_compute_consequence_bonus``): unchanged. The "num_dsbs == 0 AND bystander_severity == 0.0 → +0.03 bonus" check still triggers correctly for truly clean 0-DSB designs.

## Why this matters for the paper

Before Fix #2, the paper had to describe scoring as a two-stage process:
"compute six-dimensional TOPSIS, then apply post-hoc consequence adjustments
including bystander penalties." That framing is awkward to defend —
reviewers reasonably ask why bystanders are not in safety if they are a
safety concern, and why the fix is a "post-hoc" patch on top of a principled
MCDM method.

After Fix #2, the paper can describe scoring as a single principled
multi-criteria decision: "safety aggregates DSB-derived risk and
off-target bystander severity into one 0-1 score; feasibility, complexity,
risk, confidence, and consequence each contribute a dimension; TOPSIS
combines them." This is cleaner, easier to defend, and matches standard
MCDM practice.

**Side benefit for users.** A clinician editing outside a protein-coding
region — where bystanders do not matter functionally — can now lower the
safety weight via ``TOPSISScorer(w_safety=0.15, ...)`` to de-emphasise the
bystander contribution. Before Fix #2 they would have had to edit the
scorer implementation.

## What the CRISPRArchitect paper should now say

Paragraph-level text to use when drafting the methods section:

> **Safety.** Each strategy receives a safety score on [0, 1] derived from three components: (a) the number of double-strand breaks it induces (0 DSB = 1.0, 1 DSB = 0.6 base, 2+ DSB = 0.3 base), (b) penalties for p53-active contexts and simultaneous breaks (-0.1 each), and (c) a bystander penalty of *0.08 × bystander_severity* capturing off-target edits within the editing window. The bystander coefficient is calibrated against ABE/CBE activity profiles in protein-coding regions and is a single published constant, tunable via ``BYSTANDER_SAFETY_COEF`` in the scorer module.

> **Consequence.** A separate dimension captures splice-site proximity effects: edits near an exon-intron boundary incur a 0.05 penalty per proximity event, capped at 0.3. This dimension is orthogonal to safety and does not duplicate the bystander penalty.
