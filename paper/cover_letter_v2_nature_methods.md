# Cover Letter

**Date:** March 24, 2026

**To:** The Editor, *Nature Methods*

**Re:** Manuscript submission -- "CRISPRArchitect v2: A Transcript-Aware, Consequence-Guided Framework for Multi-Modality Genome Editing Strategy Design"

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript describing CRISPRArchitect v2, a computational framework that unifies base editing, prime editing, and HDR strategy design under a single transcript-aware, consequence-guided pipeline. We believe this work is well suited for *Nature Methods* because it addresses a methodological gap that currently forces researchers to evaluate editing modalities in isolation using separate, incompatible tools.

**The core problem.** When a researcher identifies a pathogenic variant and asks "What is the best way to correct this with CRISPR?", no existing tool provides a unified answer. Base editing designers (BE-Designer, BEable-GPS) do not evaluate prime editing alternatives; prime editing tools (PrimeDesign, pegFinder) do not consider HDR; and HDR design platforms (Benchling, CRISPOR) do not account for coding consequence or transcript context. The researcher is left to manually cross-reference multiple tools -- a process that is slow, error-prone, and does not scale.

**What CRISPRArchitect v2 does differently.** Our framework introduces three methodological advances:

1. **Transcript-aware feasibility.** For each candidate strategy, CRISPRArchitect maps the variant onto the Ensembl canonical transcript, validates the reference allele against GRCh38, annotates the coding consequence (missense, nonsense, splice-site), and verifies that a PAM-compatible guide window exists within the editing range of the chosen modality. This eliminates strategies that are theoretically possible but genomically infeasible.

2. **Consequence-guided scoring.** The multi-objective scoring function penalizes strategies that risk introducing new deleterious consequences (e.g., a base editor that corrects the target C>T but creates a bystander edit in a splice donor). This consequence-awareness is absent from all current design tools.

3. **PAM-window bottleneck quantification.** We discovered that PAM availability -- not enzymatic activity -- is the primary bottleneck limiting base editing feasibility. Across our 30-variant benchmark, approximately 40% of SNVs that are chemically amenable to base editing lack a PAM-compatible guide window. This finding has immediate practical implications for experimental design.

**Benchmark results.** We validated CRISPRArchitect v2 against 30 real pathogenic variants from ClinVar, spanning missense, nonsense, and splice-site mutations across therapeutically relevant genes (NF1, BRCA2, CFTR, DMD, TP53, and others). The framework achieves:

- **86.7% Top-1 accuracy** (the top-ranked strategy matches expert consensus)
- **96.7% Top-3 accuracy** (the correct strategy is within the top three)
- **90.0% rejection accuracy** (correctly identifies cases where no safe editing strategy exists)

These results were obtained without any training or parameter fitting on the benchmark set.

**enFnCas9 as a first-class nuclease.** CRISPRArchitect v2 includes enFnCas9, an engineered *Francisella novicida* Cas9 developed in our laboratory (Chakraborty lab, CSIR-IGIB), as a first-class nuclease option alongside SpCas9, SpCas9-NG, and xCas9. The 5'-staggered cut generated by enFnCas9 enhances HDR through extended resection, which is modeled explicitly in our gene conversion tract simulator.

**Availability.** CRISPRArchitect v2 is open-source (MIT license) and available at https://github.com/visvikbharti/CRISPRArchitect. The full pipeline runs in under 2 seconds per variant on a standard laptop with no GPU or database requirements beyond internet access for Ensembl queries.
